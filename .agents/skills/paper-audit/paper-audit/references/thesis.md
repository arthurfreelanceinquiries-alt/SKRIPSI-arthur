# Thesis and proposal review

Use this profile only for thesis/proposal work. Adapt scientific expectations to the project's stage and degree while preserving correctness.

Read the selected project overview and the relevant current institutional guidance if available. Ask about conflicting rule versions only when the conflict affects the review. The existence of a campus guide does not establish which edition or supervisor instruction applies. Cite the actual document/page for a claimed formatting or administrative requirement; otherwise label it a convention or question.

## Trace the argument

Check alignment between problem/background, research question, objective, claimed contribution, scope, literature, design, analysis, results and conclusion. For a proposal assess the planned chain through expected outputs and feasibility. Do not demand completed results, final power or observed performance that the proposal could not yet report.

Separate three kinds of advice:

- **Necessary correction:** an error, unsupported claim, or mismatch that prevents the work from answering its stated question.
- **Feasible strengthening:** an improvement that materially supports the existing question within undergraduate time, data, compute and supervision constraints.
- **Future work:** an additional cohort, theory, clinical validation or broader research program beyond the agreed scope.

Prefer a minimal defensible design to accumulating experiments. A thesis need not invent an algorithm to contribute a well-defined comparison, replication or contextual analysis. Do not promise novelty, supervisor acceptance or graduation.

## Literature and originality

Check whether the literature justifies the actual design choices and distinguishes the closest prior work. When overlap is a concern, compare concrete question, data, methods, analyses and claims. Similar topics or standard methods alone do not establish plagiarism. Ask for source passages if a citation cannot be checked. Keep the author's voice and contribution identifiable; never fabricate references to make a gap look stronger.

## Bilingual writing

Follow explicit English/Indonesian output choices. For Indonesian manuscripts, use formal, precise language and consistent technical terms. Preserve names, acronyms, equations and citation keys. Consult the supplied university style guide and authoritative language rules for consequential wording/formatting disputes; do not enforce a personal preference as a universal rule. Avoid translating a technical term differently in different chapters.

## Review result

If useful, finish with a small revision sequence ordered by dependencies: settle the question/design, repair analyses/evidence, align claims, then edit presentation. Supervisor-discussion questions should identify the actual unresolved decision and alternatives. Do not contact a supervisor or rewrite chapters unless requested.

Project-specific omics/ML topics belong in the separate biomedical/ML profile. Do not store the user's sample counts, private paths, transient experiment status or local conclusions in the reusable skill.

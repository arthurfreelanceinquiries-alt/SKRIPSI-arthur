




Technical Review of the KBMI 4 Green-Financing Manuscript
Paper-review audit
30 August 2026
Overall assessment
The manuscript addresses a relevant question and presents an unusually complete-looking set of
tables and appendices. However, its central empirical evidence is not reproducible from the data
printed in Appendix 1, and several printed observations conflict with official bank disclosures. Con-
sequently, the reported coefficients, significance tests, diagnostic conclusions, causal claims, and
policy recommendations cannot presently be relied upon. This is a major data-integrity and meth-
ods problem, not a copyediting problem. The manuscript requires reconstruction of the dataset and
complete regeneration of Chapter 4 before its substantive conclusions can be assessed.
The review inspected the full manuscript, equations, panel formulas, all 80 appendix rows, re-
ported EViews output, hypotheses, conclusions, implementation-facing syntax, regulatory state-
ments, citation integrity, and references. Sign conventions, symbol definitions, dimensions, branch
conventions, and implementation ambiguity were checked explicitly. The manuscript contains no
inverse-trigonometric or coordinate-transform formulas, so no branch or quadrant error arises in
those categories.

2. Definite error: Printed observations conflict with official financial disclosures
Location. Chapter 4 bank tables and Appendix 1, especially BBRI 2025-Q1 through 2025-Q4.
Problem. The BBRI 2025-Q4 row reports CAR 26.20%, gross NPL 2.10%, and ROA 4.15%.
BRI’s official financial publication for 31 December 2025 reports CAR 21.06%, gross NPL 3.29%,
and ROA 3.26% under its displayed bank performance-ratio basis; it also reports a separate ROA
measure of 2.64%. The official first-quarter 2025 publication reports gross NPL 3.14% and ROA
2.95%, whereas Appendix 1 gives 2.25% and 3.88%.1
Why it matters. This is not explainable by rounding. If the manuscript uses consolidated rather
than bank-only figures, a different ROA convention, or a derived measure, that basis is not identified
and still does not reconcile the displayed values. The discrepancy raises a serious provenance concern
for all 80 rows.
Suggested fix. Replace every observation from the original filings and state, for each variable,
whether it is bank-only or consolidated, period-end or period-average, quarterly or year-to-date,
and annualized or unannualized. Add a source-page column and retain a reconciliation record for
any derived ratio.

4. Unsupported claim: The design does not identify causal effects
Location. Research design; hypothesis tests; discussion; abstract and conclusions.
Problem. The manuscript describes a causal design and repeatedly says that the regressions “prove”
that Green Financing, NPL, and CAR determine ROA. The model uses contemporaneous ratios,
four selected banks, bank fixed effects only, and no identification strategy. Green Financing, risk,
capital, and profitability can be jointly determined; profitable banks may expand green lending or
capital, and common macroeconomic time trends affect all variables.
Why it matters. The appendix series are dominated by common monotonic trends: Green Fi-
nancing, CAR, and ROA generally rise while NPL falls. Bank fixed effects remove time-invariant
bank differences but do not remove common time shocks or trends. A coefficient therefore cannot
be interpreted as a causal effect or as evidence for the proposed funding-cost and debtor-quality
mechanisms.
Suggested fix. Either reframe the study consistently as associational or introduce a defensible
identification design. At minimum, estimate bank and quarter fixed effects, define lags, add justified
controls, investigate reverse causality, and report sensitivity to time trends. Mechanism claims
require measured mediators or should be presented as hypotheses rather than findings.
5. Definite error: The diagnostic conclusions are incompatible with the printed
data and use unsuitable tests
Location. Sections on stationarity and classical assumptions; Tables 4.2 and 4.4; Appendix 2.
Problem. Direct reconstruction gives a Durbin–Watson statistic near 0.566, with within-bank lag-
one residual correlations approximately 0.61–0.91, not the reported 1.942. The manuscript applies
a conventional Durbin–Watson interval to stacked panel residuals and does not test cross-sectional
dependence. It also reports LLC/IPS stationarity at level despite visibly trend-dominated series,
without providing settings, lags, deterministic terms, or raw output. Finally, normal residuals are
described as a requirement for BLUE, which they are not.
Why it matters. Serial correlation, cross-sectional dependence, and common shocks can make the
reported standard errors and p-values severely misleading. With only four bank clusters, ordinary
cluster-robust asymptotics also require caution.
3
Suggested fix. After correcting the data, report a panel serial-correlation test, a cross-sectional-
dependence diagnostic, and inference robust to the supported error structure. State unit-root speci-
fications completely and test sensitivity to deterministic trends. Avoid claiming that all assumptions
are “perfectly” satisfied.
6. Definite error: The formulas and interpretations for adjusted R2 and the FEM
F test use the wrong degrees of freedom
Location. Section “Model Feasibility and Hypothesis Testing” and the interpretation of Hypothe-
sis 4 and adjusted R2.
Problem. With N T = 80, N = 4 bank intercepts, and K = 3 slopes, the printed formula
1 − (1 − R2)(N T − 1)
N T − K − 1
ignores the bank effects. Substituting the reported R2 = 0.72514 gives 0.71429, not the reported
0.70258. The value 0.70258 is obtained when the denominator reflects all estimated parameters.
Likewise, the standard overall FEM F statistic tests the slopes together with the non-reference bank
effects; it is not automatically a test that only GF , N P L, and CAR are jointly zero.
Why it matters. Hypothesis 4 is not established by the reported statistic, and the model R2
cannot be described as the proportion explained solely by the three substantive predictors because
it also contains bank effects.
Suggested fix. Use the correct residual degrees of freedom N T − N − K for the stated parameteri-
zation. Report a separate Wald test of H0 : β1 = β2 = β3 = 0, with its numerator and denominator
degrees of freedom. Distinguish overall, within, between, and incremental explanatory power.
7. Definite error: Required notation is corrupted and key symbols change mean-
ing
Location. Variable formulas in Chapter 2; panel equations and matrix algebra in Chapter 3; Tables
4.2, 4.5, and 4.6; elasticity section; Appendix 4.
Problem. The character “?” replaces multiplication signs, inequalities, Greek letters, \left,
\multirow, and \multicolumn throughout the manuscript. More importantly, N is first defined as
the number of banks (N = 4) but is used in the Jarque–Bera formula as if it were the number of
observations. K is first the number of regressors (K = 3) but becomes kurtosis in the same formula.
In the fixed-effects discussion, the common intercept, bank-specific intercept, and bank deviation
are visually collapsed into indistinguishable corrupted symbols.
Why it matters. A literal implementation can substitute N = 4 and K = 3 into the Jarque–Bera
formula and obtain the wrong statistic. The damaged panel notation prevents an unambiguous
distinction among α, αi, µi, and β.
Suggested fix. Restore the source encoding globally, then use n = N T for sample size, N for
banks, T for periods, K for slopes, and κ for kurtosis. Define, for example, αi = α + µi and keep
those verbal descriptors unchanged everywhere.

8. Definite error: The manuscript’s multicollinearity claim is contradicted by
Appendix 1
Location. Table 4.4a, Table 4.4, and the accompanying discussion.
Problem. The manuscript reports the largest absolute correlation among regressors as 0.4120 and
centered VIFs of 1.28–1.42. Appendix 1 instead gives corr(N P L, CAR) = −0.9340. A pooled
calculation gives VIF approximately 10.05 for NPL and 8.31 for CAR; within-bank VIFs are ap-
proximately 7.0–7.3.
Why it matters. The manuscript’s assertion that multicollinearity is absent is not supported.
The highly synchronized trends make individual slope estimates unstable and complicate claims
about which variable is most important.
Suggested fix. Recompute correlations and VIFs from the final estimation sample and report
them under the same transformation used by the estimator. Address common trends and report
coefficient stability across plausible specifications.
9. Definite error: Raw coefficient magnitudes and elasticities are interpreted
incorrectly
Location. Hypothesis 2 discussion and “Partial Sensitivity and Elasticity Analysis.”
Problem. The manuscript calls NPL the most sensitive determinant because | − 0.385| is the
largest raw coefficient, although the regressors have different distributions. Later, its own reported
elasticities rank CAR above Green Financing and NPL. The elasticity values are dimensionless, but
are printed with percent signs and described as if a 1% relative increase and a one-percentage-point
increase were interchangeable.
Why it matters. This yields contradictory substantive rankings and confuses marginal effects
with elasticities.
Suggested fix. Do not rank importance from raw slopes. State that a one-percentage-point change
has the slope’s percentage-point association with ROA. For elasticity, state that a 1% relative change
in Xj is associated with an estimated ηj % relative change in ROA at the evaluation point, and do
not append a percent sign to ηj itself.
10. Likely issue: PSAK 71/IFRS 9 treatment is over-simplified and partly mis-
stated
Location. Section “Asset Quality, Collectibility, and Implementation of PSAK 71/IFRS 9” and the
NPL discussion.
Problem. The text maps regulatory collectibility mechanically onto IFRS 9 stages and suggests
that Stage 3 requires provisioning to 100% of unsecured exposure. The displayed ECL equation omits
scenario probabilities and does not clarify whether P Dt is marginal, conditional, or cumulative.
IFRS 9 recognizes probability-weighted discounted cash shortfalls and does not impose a universal
100% allowance merely because an asset is Stage 3.
Why it matters. The description can mislead readers about the mechanism connecting NPL and
CKPN and is too coarse for an implementation-facing formula.
5
Suggested fix. Check the wording against PSAK 71/IFRS 9, distinguish prudential collectibility
from accounting staging, define all ECL terms and probability conventions, and label any reduced
formula explicitly as a simplification.
11. Definite error: Regulatory descriptions contain checkable inaccuracies
Location. Chapter 1 regulatory background; Chapter 2 KUBL list; Appendix 5.
Problem. POJK 51/POJK.03/2017 does require sustainability reporting, but its reporting out-
line lists independent written verification “if any”; it does not make independent audit universal
in the way the manuscript states. The manuscript presents 11 KUBL categories and omits the
MSME category included in the relevant sustainable-business classification. It also treats POJK
60/POJK.04/2017 as the operative green-bond rule without noting its replacement by POJK 18 of
2023.2
Why it matters. These errors affect the definition of the Green Financing numerator and the
manuscript’s policy narrative.
Suggested fix. Quote the operative provisions precisely, distinguish KUBL from the broader
sustainable-business categories, state the classification used for each sample year, and update su-
perseded regulations.
12. Definite error: Citation and reference integrity is not adequate
Location. Literature review tables, in-text parenthetical keys, and “References.”
Problem. No references.bib or rendered reference list is present, although the manuscript
ends with \bibliography{references}. The strings such as (Pramono2022) are not valid LaTeX
citation commands. Spot checks also reveal title/content mismatches: Cui et al. (2018) is The
Impact of Green Lending on Credit Risk in China, not the title printed in Table 2.1; Chiaramonte
et al. (2020) is an insurance-industry article, not the bank study claimed there; and the Yin et al.
(2021) article has a different title and materially more heterogeneous findings than the summary
reports.3
Why it matters. The claimed research gap and theoretical support cannot be evaluated when
citations are absent, misidentified, or summarized inaccurately.
Suggested fix. Build a complete bibliography from DOI/publisher metadata, convert every citation
to \cite or the chosen author–year command, and verify each table row against the actual article’s
sample, outcome, method, and conclusion.
